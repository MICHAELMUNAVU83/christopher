1. Overall Website Design and Visual Direction

The website should be redesigned to have a more modern, professional, engaging and AI-inspired visual identity that communicates the mission, vision and impact of CM.

The overall design should use AI-generated silhouette illustrations, pencil-drawn artwork and meaningful visual representations to communicate the organisation's goals, rather than relying on photographs throughout the website.

The illustrations should reflect African people, communities, young people, talent development, sports, empowerment and sustainable community development. Where possible, the drawings should have an African identity and represent the people and communities CM serves.

Key design recommendation:

Use AI-inspired silhouette images and pencil-sketch illustrations across the website to communicate the purpose of each section.
Avoid using photographs on the homepage and other informational pages.
Reserve actual photographs exclusively for the Gallery, where the organisation's activities, programmes and achievements will be showcased.
Maintain a consistent visual identity, with coordinated colours, typography, spacing and illustration styles.
Ensure the website is responsive and works well on mobile phones, tablets and desktop computers.
The intention is to make the website visually appealing while keeping the focus on CM's mission, programmes and impact.

3. Homepage: Communicating the Identity and Purpose of CM

The homepage should immediately communicate what CM stands for, what it does and the change it seeks to create.

Instead of filling the homepage with photographs, use AI-generated African-inspired silhouette illustrations and pencil-drawn visuals that represent the organisation's key areas of work.

The visual direction should communicate:

Community empowerment and development.
Talent identification and nurturing.
Sports and youth development.
Education, opportunity and personal growth.
Sustainability and community self-reliance.
The homepage should include a clear introduction to CM, a concise summary of its mission and vision, an overview of its main objectives and prominent links to the organisation's programmes.

Suggested calls to action include Learn More About Us, Explore Our Programmes and Get Involved.

4. About Us: Expand the Organisation's Story

The existing Learn More About Us section should be reviewed to determine whether it provides enough information about the foundation and its purpose.

This section should provide more than a brief introduction. It should help visitors understand the organisation, its background, its aspirations and the people behind its establishment.

The About Us page should include the following subsections:

4.1 Who We Are

Provide a clear explanation of what CM is, why it was established, the community needs it seeks to address and the difference it aims to make.

4.2 Our Mission

Clearly state the organisation's mission, explaining what CM exists to do and the people or communities it seeks to serve.

4.3 Our Vision

Explain the long-term change CM hopes to achieve and the future it seeks to help create.

4.4 Who Was CM?

The information currently presented under the heading Who Was CM? should be incorporated into the About Us page rather than treated as a separate main navigation item.

This section should explain the person, story or inspiration behind CM, including the values and legacy that inform its work. The content should be based on verified information supplied by the organisation.

Recommendation: The About Us page should establish the organisation's identity, while its programmes and objectives should explain how it puts its mission into practice.

5. Our Objectives: Simplify the Text and Strengthen the Visuals

The current objectives should be reviewed and consolidated into one clear, comprehensive sentence that communicates the organisation's main areas of focus.

The objectives should reflect talent development, sports, empowerment, sustainability and community self-reliance.

Suggested draft objective:

"To nurture talent and promote sports development, empower individuals and communities, and advance sustainable initiatives that strengthen skills, create opportunities and foster long-term self-reliance."

This sentence should be refined to reflect CM's officially approved objectives.

Visual presentation

Rather than using numerous photographs or lengthy blocks of text, each key area of work should be represented through a simple AI-generated silhouette or pencil-drawn illustration.

For example:

Talent Development: An illustration of a young African person exploring their potential and developing their skills.
Sports Development: An illustration of African youth participating in sporting activities.
Community Empowerment: An illustration of people learning, collaborating and building opportunities together.
Sustainability: An illustration representing agriculture, environmental stewardship, practical skills or community-led development.
These illustrations should follow one consistent artistic style so that the website feels unified and professionally designed.

A dedicated Stay in Touch section should be introduced to help CM maintain communication with supporters, partners, volunteers and other interested visitors.

Suggested heading:

Stay in Touch With Us

Suggested introductory text:

"Stay connected with CM and be the first to hear about our programmes, upcoming activities, community initiatives, opportunities and the impact we are creating together. Subscribe to receive updates and news directly in your inbox."

The section should include:

An email address input field.
A clearly visible Subscribe button.
A brief privacy notice explaining how subscribers' email addresses will be used.
A suitable confirmation message after successful subscription.

The purpose of this section is to build a lasting relationship with the people who want to follow CM's work and receive future updates.

10. AI-Generated Illustrations: Recommended Artistic Direction

The illustrations should form a recognisable visual identity throughout the website.

The preferred style is a combination of:

African-inspired human silhouettes.
Hand-drawn pencil sketches.
Minimalist line illustrations.
Soft shading and carefully selected accent colours.
Symbolic representations of talent, sports, empowerment and sustainability.
The illustrations should be meaningful rather than merely decorative. Each image should help explain the purpose of the section in which it appears.

For example, the talent development section could feature an African-inspired pencil illustration of a young person discovering their abilities, while the sustainability section could use a drawing representing community farming, environmental care or practical skills development.

The images should be original or appropriately licensed, culturally respectful and consistent in style.

The developer should ensure that the illustrations do not obscure text, reduce readability or make the website unnecessarily heavy to load. Optimised image formats and appropriate alternative text should be used.

11. Website Content, Usability and Technical Improvements

Alongside the visual redesign, the following improvements are recommended:

Clear content hierarchy: Give every page a descriptive title, clear subheadings and concise paragraphs.
Consistent navigation: Ensure that visitors can move easily between About Us, Objectives, Programmes, Gallery and Get Involved.
Mobile responsiveness: Ensure that text, illustrations, navigation and forms display correctly on smaller screens.
Accessibility: Use readable font sizes, sufficient colour contrast, descriptive links and alternative text for meaningful illustrations.
Performance: Compress and optimise illustrations and Gallery photographs to maintain fast loading times.
Working links and forms: Test all navigation links, the online store, the email subscription form and other calls to action.
Search visibility: Use descriptive page titles, meta descriptions and relevant headings to help visitors discover the organisation through search engines.
